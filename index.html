body

Normal:

jamInd low and stable (20-40), jammingState = 1 (ok)

CN0 values spread out across satellites (e.g., 20–45 dBHz) — because real satellites are at different elevations/distances, so they're never all the same strength

Position/clock drifting smoothly, not jumping

numSV and DOP varying slowly over time as satellites rise/set

Jamming

jamInd rises sharply, jammingState is warning/critial

AGC (agcCnt) jumps as the receiver tries to compensate for a raised noise floor

CN0 across the board drops or fix is lost entirely (fixType degrades)

Spoofing

Unnaturally uniform CN0, e.g. every satellite suddenly reports near-identical signal strength, suggesting a spoofer is using one transmitter faking multiple "satellites," so they arrive with correlated power rather than the natural spread.

Position or clock jump inconsistent with motion/velocity — a static receiver's position "teleporting," or time suddenly skipping, especially if it happens without a corresponding jamming/interference flag (a well-crafted spoof doesn't need to jam anything — it can just overpower the real signal cleanly).

jamInd/jammingState looking totally normal while the solution is behaving oddly — this contrast is itself a signal. Real GNSS shouldn't produce impossible jumps quietly; if the interference monitor is clean but the position/clock isn't, that's a mismatch worth flagging.

Sudden, implausible improvement in DOP or satellite count — more satellites appearing at once than plausible for real sky geometry, or DOP becoming unnaturally good.

Carrier-phase/pseudorange inconsistency (needs RXM-RAWX) — the gold-standard check used in the research literature, but a stretch goal for later.
